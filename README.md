# BayBlade

**BayBlade** is the working codename for a hardcore physics-driven spinning-top engineering and career simulation.

The fantasy is simple:

> Start as a random person with cheap plastic toy parts and hand tools. End as a terrifyingly competent engineer running a precision workshop and building near-future competition machines that can survive absurd rotational speeds and catastrophic impacts.

This is not an arcade stat game. There is no hidden "Attack 92" number doing the real work. Performance should emerge from geometry, material properties, mass distribution, tolerances, manufacturing quality, launch conditions, wear, thermal state, damage, and physics.

## Core pillars

- **Physics first.** Once launched, the player has no direct control over the top.
- **Engineering is gameplay.** Designing, manufacturing, measuring, testing, diagnosing, and iterating are the primary skill expressions.
- **Everything can fail.** Fatigue, cracks, bearing wear, fastener loosening, heat, deformation, debris, and catastrophic structural failure are real parts of the simulation.
- **Knowledge is earned.** The player only sees data their current instruments can actually measure or infer.
- **Progression is physical.** New capabilities come from tools, machines, materials, expertise, suppliers, and better simulation rather than abstract "+10% engineering" upgrades.
- **The world evolves with the player.** Rivals have careers, budgets, workshops, designs, failures, suppliers, and strategies of their own.
- **The simulator knows physics, not mercy.** Dangerous or stupid designs are warned against, not forbidden.

## Career arc

1. Premade plastic junk, hand tools, local events.
2. Garage tinkering, manual modifications, salvaged parts.
3. Precision measuring tools, better suppliers, custom parts.
4. Lathes, mills, heat treatment, balancing, proper testing.
5. CNC, advanced materials, automated workflows, simulation.
6. Professional competition, sponsorships, manufacturing, market influence.
7. Endgame engineering laboratory: custom assemblies, exotic materials, near-future manufacturing, deep failure analysis, open-ended competition.

## Main competition rule

The primary career mode does **not** use score-based judging.

A match ends when one competitor is eliminated by a physically meaningful result such as:

- spin death,
- ring-out,
- mechanical incapacitation,
- catastrophic structural failure.

Side leagues and special events may use alternative rules.
